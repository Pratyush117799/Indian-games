Raja — राजा
नृप  ·  King  ·  Chaturanga
Three SVG prototypes for the Raja piece. Each is a circular disc depicting a bearded Indian king in royal attire — frontal, dignified, unmistakably royal. The oversized pagdi turban or pointed kireet crown is the primary piece-type signal, distinct from every warrior piece in the set at any board size.
Prototype 1 Pagdi Raja — पगड़ी राजा
The Turbaned King  ·  8-petal royal lotus ring  ·  Sarpech jewel + Talwar sword
Frontal-facing Rajput maharaja. The oversized wrapped pagdi — far taller and wider than any soldier's headgear — is the instant king signal. The sarpech jeweled ornament peaks from the turban crown. Full rounded beard, shoulder disc ornaments, and a talwar sword held at the chest complete the portrait. The 8-petal royal lotus border uses wide petals (rx=5.5, ry=8.5), noticeably more opulent than the Ashwa's 12 thinner petals, giving the Raja a heavier, more regal ring presence.

Integration

Drop-in replacement in renderBoardGrid(). The viewBox is identical (80×80) to Ashwa and Nara. Set the fill on the figure <g> to playerColours[owner] — a single value drives the entire piece. At 32 px the large turban dome above a wide beard reads as a wide-shouldered portrait silhouette, fundamentally unlike the Ashwa horse-head and all Nara action stances. Render at width="50" height="50" inside a 54 px square; the viewBox handles all internal scaling.
Prototype 2 Mukut Samrat — मुकुट सम्राट
The Crowned Emperor  ·  Pearl bead chain ring  ·  Kireet crown + Danda scepter
An emperor in a pointed kireet mukut crown — five ornate spires rising from a jeweled base, the most visually distinct headgear silhouette of the three prototypes. The danda scepter held upright in the left hand is unique in the entire piece set; no soldier carries a vertical staff. A large chest medallion and the scepter-staff together create a strong cruciform composition readable at 32 px. The pearl-bead border (20 spheres) directly references the royal haar necklace — an emblem unique to royalty.

Integration

The upright danda creates a vertical axis that collapses cleanly at 32 px — it reads as a pillar beside the figure at minimum board size, unlike any weapon or shield shape among the Nara prototypes. The five crown spires break slightly above the inner disc rim, creating a unique crown-notch silhouette visible even at 40 px. Pearl border circles are 20 × <circle> elements — lightest DOM of the three borders. Same width="50" height="50" on 54 px squares; one fill variable per army.
Prototype 3 Maharaja Mayur — महाराजा मयूर
The Peacock King  ·  12-point Surya ray ring  ·  Peacock plume + Raised Talwar
A Maratha-style warrior maharaja — the regal pagdi turban adorned with a peacock-feather plume (mayur pankh) that breaks the upper-right of the disc, creating an asymmetric silhouette no soldier carries. The sword is raised high in battle-ready posture, combining king signals (sarpech jewel, full beard, kantha necklace) with martial energy. The 12-point Surya ray border echoes the Indian solar emblems on Mauryan pillars and the Ashoka wheel — a symbol of sovereign power unlike any Nara border.

Integration

The feather eye extends to approximately r=33 from disc centre, visible as a teardrop notch above the border ring at 54–90 px; it merges into the turban dome at 32 px with no visual confusion. The raised sword diagonal points upper-right — opposite to the Nara archer's bow — ensuring zero silhouette ambiguity between the two most common piece types on the board. Surya border: 12 <polygon> elements — same DOM weight as the trishul border on Nara P3. Render at width="50" height="50" inside a 54 px square.
