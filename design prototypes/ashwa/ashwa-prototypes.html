Three Prototypes — The Ashwa
अश्व · The War Horse
Three design philosophies. Each tested at all four player colours and multiple board sizes. All use --pc (player colour), --pc2 (highlight), --pc3 (accent) — swapped by a single class or inline style on the parent element.
Prototype A · Takka — The Coin
A · Takka — The Coin
ताम्र · Medallion Format
Circular coin format, closest to the reference images. A rearing horse in cream with full Rajasthani ceremony: lotus-petal border ring (player colour), jhool saddle cloth with army emblem, three ghungroo bells, maang-tikka forehead ornament, kohl-lined eye, nathuni nose ring. The horse body is always neutral cream — player colour identity comes entirely from the decorative ring and cloth layer. This is exactly how chess.com pieces work: the shape is fixed, the tint lives in details.

Integration — Prototype A (Medallion)
Size on board
viewBox 100×100 circular. At a 54px square: set width="50" height="50" — 93% fill with 2px breathing room each side. The circle never overflows regardless of scaling. At 40px squares: width="37" height="37".
Colour system
Set style="--pc:#c0392b; --pc2:#f87171" on a parent wrapper div, or inline on the SVG. Every decorative element (petals, jhool, tikka, bells, emblem) reads from var(--pc) and var(--pc2). The horse body uses fixed CSS vars --hb and --ho defined in root.
clipPath uniqueness
Each piece SVG needs a unique clipPath id. Use a counter or square coordinate: id="clip-r3c5". In ui.js renderBoardGrid: generate id="clip-"+sq where sq is the square label (e.g. "e4"). Duplicate IDs in the DOM cause all clips to reference the first one.
Army emblems on the jhool: Red = Surya chakra (sun wheel with 8 spokes), Blue = river wave (Ganga), Green = peacock eye (Parvati/Karttikeya), Yellow = Surya rays with central disc. When building other piece types (Raja, Ratha, Danti, Nara), the same emblem can repeat on each piece's cloth panel — giving each army a heraldic identity across all 5 types.
Prototype B · Kshatriya — The Silhouette
B · Kshatriya — The Silhouette
क्षत्रिय · Carved Stone Method
Full player-colour silhouette — identical philosophy to chess.com. The whole piece is one colour with lighter and darker internal paths for depth. Reads cleanly at 32px. Indian character comes from: maang-tikka crescent (48px+), kohl-lined eye, mane flow, lotus pedestal base, jhool collar with three ornament dots. The rearing horse facing left follows universal chess knight convention — so players instinctively understand it as a horse piece from any distance.

Integration — Prototype B (Silhouette)
Size on board
viewBox 44×78 (w×h). At 54px square: width="48" height="48". At 40px: width="36" height="36". At 32px: width="28" height="28" — still reads clearly as a horse. No clipPath needed — the piece never overflows its viewBox.
Drop into ui.js
In renderBoardGrid(), replace the Unicode span with an inline SVG element. Use innerHTML to inject the SVG string, setting the fill attributes to the player's hex colour directly. No CSS vars needed — avoids inheritance issues in legacy browsers.
Minimum readable size
The ear tip silhouette and rearing front leg are distinguishable at 20px rendered width — smaller than any Chaturanga board will ever use. At 36px the eye reads. At 48px the tikka and collar ornaments appear. Detail scales with size naturally.
This prototype is the recommended choice for the main game board where square sizes may drop to 40px on mobile. The silhouette approach is universally understood — every chess player from any tradition reads a rearing horse as a knight/horse piece. The Indian detail layers over that recognisability rather than replacing it.
Prototype C · Hoysala — The Relief
C · Hoysala — The Relief
होयसल · Temple Stone Carving
Inspired by Hoysala and Vijayanagara temple carvings. The horse body is always cream (fixed stone) — the heavy player-colour outline carries all army identity. Maximum contrast across all four armies. The decorative vocabulary is strictly geometric and carved: chain-link neck band, rosette cheek ornament, tiered stepped pedestal with lotus ring, scrolled mane as outline strokes. At distance, the piece reads as a bold line drawing in one clear colour — at close range the Indian ornament vocabulary emerges.

Integration — Prototype C (Hoysala Relief)
Size on board
viewBox 60×90 — taller than wide like a temple panel. At 54px square: width="50" height="50" fills the square with the pedestal touching the bottom edge. The body occupies the upper ~70% of the square — the natural visual weight zone for a chess piece. Stroke weight is baked into viewBox coordinates and scales automatically.
Why outline carries colour
The horse body is always var(--hb) (cream/parchment). The 2.5px stroke is the player colour. This means on both light and dark board squares, the piece is identifiable by its outline ring — not confused with either square colour. Red outline on dark oak squares reads perfectly; cream fill prevents the piece vanishing into the light squares.
Accessibility advantage
Because army identity is carried by outline weight rather than fill, this prototype is the most accessible option for colour-blind players. The thick stroke remains distinguishable even when hue is removed. For a game with 4 armies, this matters — two armies always share similar luminance ranges (e.g. Red and Green on dark squares).
The geometric ornament vocabulary (chain links, tiered rectangles, rosette circles) is directly lifted from Hoysala pillar capitals at Belur and Halebidu. The same vocabulary can be applied consistently to all 5 piece types — Raja with crown steps, Ratha with wheel spokes, Danti with tusk arcs — creating a visually unified army that reads as carved from the same temple wall.

Choose A if
You want to match the reference image aesthetic exactly. Board squares are 52px or larger. You want army-unique emblems (sun/wave/peacock/rays) on each piece's cloth.
Choose B if
You need mobile support with small squares (down to 32px). You want the simplest integration into ui.js. You want chess.com's proven visual language with Indian character added on top.
Choose C if
Maximum visual contrast matters most. You have colour-blind users in your player base. You want a single coherent visual language for all 5 piece types rooted in one historical art tradition.
