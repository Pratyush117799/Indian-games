The History

800 Years of Wisdom

Moksha Patam is not a children's game. It is a dharmic teaching instrument — a map of consequence painted by saints, played by kings, and stripped of meaning by empire. We are restoring what was taken.


The British renamed it "Snakes and Ladders" in 1892. They replaced karma with thrift, moksha with Victorian virtue, and asymmetry with balance. In 1943, Milton Bradley turned the snakes into slides. Each step further from the source.


2nd Century BCE

Earliest Known References

Morality-teaching board games referenced in Sanskrit texts. Conceptual roots of the tradition.

13th Century CE

Sant Jnaneshwar, Maharashtra

Attributed creator of the formalised 72-square version. Used as a tool for teaching karma and dharma to children.

16th Century

Jain & Vaishnava Variants

84-square Jain cosmological version. Vaikunthapali tradition in Telugu-speaking regions. The game becomes a living, regional tradition.

1892

Colonial Erasure

Game brought to England. Spiritual philosophy stripped and replaced with Victorian moral codes. Renamed "Snakes and Ladders."

2026

Krida — Restoration Begins

The original philosophy, art, and regional traditions restored to digital form. Played globally, understood authentically.
