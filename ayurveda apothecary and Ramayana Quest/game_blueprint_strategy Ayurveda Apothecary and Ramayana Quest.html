Core concept: "The Healing Apothecary"
You are an apprentice Vaidya (Ayurvedic healer). Your guru Charaka assigns you patients each day. You must diagnose their dosha imbalance, go to your living herb garden, harvest the right plants, grind them in your mortar and pestle, and prepare the remedy. Get it right → patient heals → you unlock deeper herb lore and new recipes.
Visual style
Vintage botanical illustration
Hand-inked herb drawings with watercolour washes. Aged parchment card backs. Ornate Sanskrit-lettered borders. Colour palette: saffron, forest green, turmeric gold, clay red, ink black. Looks like a 200-year-old Ayurvedic manuscript come to life.
Core loop (3 minutes per session)
Diagnose → Harvest → Craft → Heal
1. Patient arrives with symptoms
2. Dosha quiz (Vata / Pitta / Kapha)
3. Herb garden: pick ingredients
4. Mortar & pestle crafting mini-game
5. Remedy administered → story outcome
6. Herb fact card unlocked
Living herb garden
Changes with device date/time. Monsoon = tulsi + ginger flourishing. Summer = aloe + rose. Winter = ashwagandha + turmeric. Plants grow in real time. You water them daily. This drives daily return.
Mortar & pestle mini-game
Click-and-drag ingredients into bowl. Circular grinding motion (mouse/touch) activates. Speed + duration affects remedy potency. Particles swirl. Colour changes as ingredients combine. Deeply satisfying for all ages.
Dosha personality quiz
Children discover their own dosha constitution (Vata / Pitta / Kapha). Their character in-game reflects this — different starting herbs, different strengths. "You are Pitta — fiery, sharp-minded" etc. Personal = engaging.
100 herb compendium
Each herb has: Sanskrit name, regional names (5 languages), botanical illustration, properties, season, and a fun fact. Unlocked through gameplay. Becomes a collectible encyclopedia. Printable phygital cards link back to game.
Patient story quests
30+ illustrated patient encounters with branching outcomes. A farmer with joint pain. A child who can't sleep. An elder with digestion issues. Moral choices woven in — do you give the expensive rare herb or the accessible common one?
Multiplayer herb bazaar
Online trade/barter market. Players grow herbs their region specialises in and trade for others. Seasonal scarcity creates real economics. Family co-op: split roles (one diagnoses, one harvests, one crafts).
Key animations
Herb particles float when harvesting Card flip with parchment texture Mortar grinding with colour swirl Day/night + seasonal garden cycle Remedy bottle filling animation Patient healing glow effect Herb compendium page-turn Guru Charaka floating dialogue
Core concept: "The Living Ramayana"
This is NOT a retelling. It's a moral consequence engine. You play AS Rama (or choose Sita, Hanuman, Lakshmana). Every decision you make — spare or fight, trust or doubt, act swiftly or with patience — echoes forward. Characters you show mercy to appear later as allies. It makes the Ramayana feel like YOUR story.
Visual style
Amar Chitra Katha reimagined
Bold black outlines, flat jewel-tone colours (saffron, royal blue, forest green, gold). Panoramic scene panels like comic pages. Characters have expressive eyes. Location art uses parallax depth — clouds, forest layers, ocean waves move as you scroll. Iconic, nostalgic, instantly recognisable.
Moral consequence engine
Choices ripple forward
Choice A: Spare Vibhishana → he becomes key ally in Lanka
Choice B: Trust Shabari's berries → she gives you a secret path
Choice C: Send Lakshmana back patiently → he arrives in time
Each Kanda (chapter) remembers your decisions from the last
Journey map (7 Kandas)
Bala → Ayodhya → Aranya → Kishkindha → Sundara → Yuddha → Uttara. Animated map with sea, forest, mountains. Each location unlocks on completion. Real geography overlaid on ancient India map.
Virtue points system
Dharma (right action), Courage, Devotion, Wisdom. Every choice shifts your balance. Your character's aura colour changes. High Dharma unlocks Rama's divine bow powers. High Devotion gives Hanuman special moves.
Mini-games at each location
Ayodhya: Lift Shiva's bow (physics puzzle). Panchavati: Build Parna Kuti (Tetris-like). Kishkindha: Sugriva's strength trial. Lanka: Bridge-building with floating stones. Each teaches a story beat through play.
Animated cutscenes
Illustrated panels animate like a motion comic. Hanuman flying is a parallax scroll sequence. Ravana's palace — golden towers, swaying palms. Rendered in Amar Chitra Katha style with subtle CSS animation. No video files needed.
Knowledge challenges
After each mini-game: 3 questions about the Ramayana moment you just played. Wrong answer = guru explains with context. Right answer = Sanskrit shloka revealed with pronunciation. Earns Sanskriti Score points.
Character abilities
Rama: precision + dharma. Lakshmana: loyalty shield (protects others). Hanuman: strength + flight traversal. Sita: inner wisdom (sees through illusion). Each plays differently — encourages replay.
Ideas that will win the hackathon
🌱
Seasonal living garden (real-world time) — The herb garden in Ayurveda Apothecary reads your device's date. In June-September (monsoon), tulsi, ginger, and pepper grow abundantly. In winter, ashwagandha, sesame, and turmeric. In summer, aloe, rose, and amla. Players are incentivised to return at different times of year to access different herbs. No other educational game does this.
🎯
Phygital printable cards — Every herb card and Ramayana character card can be printed from the app. Each has a QR code that, when scanned, launches the game with that herb/character highlighted. Physical cards act as "save points" and "cheat codes." Children naturally carry them around, show friends, and recruit new players. Zero manufacturing cost.
🔬
Camera herb scanner (PWA) — Using the device camera + a lightweight plant recognition model (or shape-matching heuristic), players can point their phone at real plants in their garden or kitchen. Identified plants appear in their in-game herb compendium. Turmeric root in the kitchen? Scan it → unlock the turmeric card. Brings the physical world into the game.
📊
Sanskriti Score + Parent Dashboard — One number (0–1000) that represents how much Indian cultural knowledge a child has absorbed across both games. Parents see a weekly report: "Priya learned about 8 herbs, identified 3 doshas, completed Kishkindha Kanda, and earned the Scholar of Tulsi badge." This is the feature that gets schools to adopt it.
🗣️
Voice narration in 5 languages — Guru Charaka narrates herb facts in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and English. Valmiki narrates Ramayana passages. Children can switch languages mid-game. Exposes them to other Indian languages naturally. Uses the Web Speech API (zero cost) with text-to-speech, upgraded to recorded audio for demo.
🤖
AI Guru mode (via Anthropic API) — Children can ask Sage Charaka questions: "Why is ashwagandha good for sleep?" "What did Hanuman eat?" The game routes these to Claude with a cultural-accuracy system prompt. The AI stays in character, answers in age-appropriate language, and always ties answers back to the game. This is the demo moment that makes judges gasp.
🎨
Warli / Madhubani art mode — An accessibility toggle that renders the entire game in Warli tribal art style (white stick figures on terracotta) or Madhubani (intricate line-art flowers and geometric patterns). Same game, completely different visual experience. Showcases India's art diversity. Unique in any game, anywhere.
